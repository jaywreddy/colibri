## 1. Optical patterns — 2D audit

Method: for each of the 17 registered patterns I drove the real backend
`/sim/parallax2d` endpoint at **physics-derived tilt angles** taken from each
manifest (not arbitrary angles), then checked both the numbers and the images.
Barrier switches were correlated against their own `view_a`/`view_b` reference
images **restricted to the support where those two images actually differ**, so
the shared slit grating cannot inflate the score. Carrier reveals were probed at
θ(shift = p/2) computed from the manifest's carrier period and substrate.

### Working correctly

**Barrier switches — all four pass, with textbook anti-symmetry.** Correlation
against the intended views flips sign cleanly across the design angle:

| Pattern | half-angle | −tilt: corr A / B | +tilt: corr A / B |
|---|---|---|---|
| gear-quill-switch | ±2.51° | +0.271 / −0.271 | −0.282 / +0.282 |
| globe-duo-phase | ±2.51° | +0.234 / −0.234 | −0.231 / +0.231 |
| jp-monogram-phase | ±2.51° | +0.366 / −0.366 | −0.296 / +0.296 |
| colibri-flap-phase | ±2.51° | +0.170 / −0.170 | −0.171 / +0.171 |

The sign structure is exactly what a parallax barrier should produce, and the
construction is correct per the project contract (both images interlaced in the
back layer, slit mask in front). Absolute magnitudes are modest (0.17–0.37) —
consistent with a 40%-duty slit barrier, not a defect.

**monogram-carrier-reveal — the strongest performer.** "J + P" is fully legible
head-on and genuinely disappears at ±3.35° (corr 0 vs ±θ = −0.32 / −0.34, mean
transmission dropping 65.8 → 57.3). This is the reveal working as designed.

**Moirés — fringes move.** wayuu-kanasü (corr 0.63 → 0.53 over two steps),
emerald-facet (0.86 → 0.67), colibri-globe (0.81 → 0.71), orchid-shimmer
(0.31 → −0.04, the liveliest). Both lenticular stereo pairs also respond
(colibri-globe-lenticular and globe-rotation-stereo, ~0.89 → 0.81).

### Findings

**(A) Four patterns have a completely empty back mask and are exactly
tilt-invariant.** `inscription-line`, `jamon-tray`, `monogram-jp` and
`food-pair-chirp` all ship `back.png` with mean = 0.0000 and std = 0.0000 —
literally no gold on the far surface. Their parallax response is not weak, it is
**identically zero**: corr(0°, ±θ) = **+1.000000** at their own reveal angle, and
mean transmission does not move in the fourth digit.

This matters because their own manifest descriptions promise motion — jamon-tray
says the scene "shimmers with a moiré highlight as the piece tilts",
monogram-jp says "the letters shimmer with a moiré highlight as the lid tilts".
With nothing in the back layer there is no second grating to beat against, and
per the project's own honesty rule a front-layer mask does not move with tilt, so
no parallax effect of any kind can occur. (Two of the four descriptions do also
say the back is left plain, so the *geometry* is intentional — it is the claimed
*optical behaviour* that the geometry cannot deliver.)

Worth noting these are not necessarily bad *physical* parts: a 20 µm front-only
gold line screen on quartz will give real specular and diffractive colour play in
the hand, and food-pair-chirp's steam tips sweep to 4.5 µm which is squarely
diffractive. But that is a different effect from moiré/parallax, the 2D simulator
cannot show it, and the copy currently describes the wrong mechanism.

Decision needed: either give these four a real back carrier (making them true
moiré pieces), or relabel them as diffraction/specular pieces and stop promising
tilt shimmer.

**(B) `bitmap-halftone` has no declared parallax axis, and its live axis is the
one tooling won't guess.** Swept in x it is inert (corr 0.999 at 1.67°); swept in
**y** it is one of the liveliest patterns in the catalog (corr 0.77 → 0.54 → 0.35,
mean 87.7 → 115.0). It is a horizontal line screen, so y is correct — but nothing
in the manifest says so, so a generic sweep (mine, and the Pattern Lab's default)
reports a working pattern as dead. Recommend emitting a `parallax_axis_deg` (or
reusing `switch_axis_deg`) in `recipe_data` for the halftone family.

**(C) `capybara-scanimation` — the capybara itself does not animate, and the
phase order does not advance monotonically.** Across the full designed cycle
(0° → 10.04° in 2.51° steps) consecutive frames correlate at 0.94–0.95 and the
cycle closes at 0.938. Visually, the capybara silhouette is *identical* in all
five frames; only the water band below the waterline changes. Correlating each
composite against the four reference `phase_N.png` images gives best matches in
the order phase_0 → phase_3 → phase_3 → phase_2, i.e. not 0→1→2→3.

The reference-image correlations are all low and closely spaced (0.18–0.27), so
the ordering evidence is suggestive rather than conclusive — but combined with a
static body it is worth a look. If a static body above `waterline: 0.66` is
intended, the pattern name oversells it.

## 2. Optical patterns — 3D audit

The `@effects` suite passes 8/8 against the live WebGL buffer, including the two
checks that actually matter for honesty: the inner plane sits at exactly the
paraxial T/n gap (0.342466 mm measured vs 0.342466 mm expected for T = 500 µm,
n = 1.46), and collapsing that gap kills the cross-layer effect
(changedFrac 0.15 collapse, 0.45 on the carrier reveal).

My own independent 3D probe — camera placed analytically along the front face's
world normal, front face bound to `gear-quill-switch` through the real store →
backend → texture pipeline — **could not resolve the switch** at ±2.51°
(mad 0.06–0.13, corr 1.0000 even with the plane gap collapsed). That is a
limitation of my probe, not a renderer bug: at 60 µm the barrier is sub-pixel at
any sane viewing distance, which the app itself documents in the Preview panel
("at 1× they are sub-pixel and deliberately fade to flat gold — only camera zoom
or a higher device pixel ratio resolves them"). The suite's equivalent probe uses
a much harder zoom (`zoomForMicroPatterns`, radius ×0.45 and DPR 2.5) and does
measure the flip (changedFrac 0.26 across ±14°). Reported here so the negative
result isn't mistaken for a defect.

**(D) High-consequence UI truth bug: the fab-warning label contradicts the
backend.** The Build rail renders, in error red:

> ⚠ front.svg baked the comb at 874 μm → **≈ ±32.5°** *on the fabricated part*

The 874 µm figure is real — `svg_bake_barrier_period_um` in the cached plate
manifests confirms the SVG raster coarsens the 60 µm barrier by ~14.6× to fit the
cell budget. But "on the fabricated part" is **wrong**. The fabricated part comes
from `fine.gds`, and `export_fine.build_plate_fine` reads
`fab_center_period_um` = 60.0 exactly — its docstring says so explicitly ("the
TRUE optical values, not the coarse budget rescale"). The export bundle's own
README is equally clear: `fine.gds` is "THE fab-grade mask", and
`front.svg`/`back.svg` are "PREVIEWS, not masks … do NOT write gold from them."

So the masks are correct at ±2.5°; only the throwaway preview SVG is coarse. As
written, the UI tells an optical engineer their part will switch at ±32.5° — which
is the kind of message that halts a tapeout. The fix is wording, not physics:
attribute the number to the preview SVG, and drop the error styling (or point at
the fine.gds realized period from `FINE_MASKS.json` instead).

## 3. Frontend ease-of-use audit

Five dimensions audited in parallel and each finding verified against source
before it survived. 24 raw findings → 14 confirmed. Highest-impact:

1. **No persistence of the live design.** `store.ts` uses no `persist`
   middleware and there is no `beforeunload` guard; the only thing in
   localStorage is section open/closed state (`kit.tsx:116`). A refresh discards
   every face assignment and frame tweak — minutes of cold backend generation —
   unless a preset was saved first.
2. **"Reset to defaults" destroys all six faces in one unconfirmed click**
   (`BuildPanel.tsx:596-606`), with no undo anywhere in the store.
3. **Saving a preset silently overwrites a colliding slug.** `savePreset`
   (`App.tsx:399`) never checks `savedBoxes`, and `materialize_box`
   (`boxes.py:266`) does `mkdir(exist_ok=True)` and clobbers `box.json`.
4. **Pattern catalog load failure is invisible** — the boot retry loop
   (`App.tsx:296-325`) only `log()`s, never touching `setError`/`setBusy`, so a
   down backend shows an empty pattern grid indistinguishable from "broken".
5. **Four real frame parameters have no UI at all** — `edge_gradient`,
   `understory`, `border_vine`, `corner_fans` are in `FrameSpec` with distinct
   per-face values in `FACE_FRAME_PROFILE` (`api.ts:196-215, 350-396`) but no
   control anywhere.
6. **First box generate shows six black squares** — `FacesPanel.tsx:38-75` has no
   placeholder while `boxManifest` is null, during the slowest op in the app
   (there is already a `Shimmer` in `kit.tsx` to use).
7. **No responsive breakpoints** — hard `100vh/100vw` grid with
   `280px 1fr 340px` columns, a non-wrapping header, and a fixed 470px Pattern
   Lab overlay; no `@media` anywhere in `frontend/src`.
8. **Async status is invisible to assistive tech** — regen status, regen error,
   export progress and the download confirmation carry no `aria-live`/`role`;
   the header's preset input and preset select have no accessible name.
9. Tuning is discarded on pattern switch and Pattern Lab close, with no dirty
   flag or per-(face, slug) param cache.

Nothing here is implemented — it is a report, as requested.

## 4. 3D render fidelity — implemented

Landed as commit `3efe23b` (merge of a 10-commit branch + 3 fixes of mine).

- **Glass**: `roughness` 0.06 → 0.012, `transmission` 0.85 → 0.94. `roughness`
  drives three's transmission blur LOD directly and the inner gold plane is only
  ever seen *through* this material, so it was the last low-pass filter on the
  back layer (lod ≈ 0.58 → 0.115). The 15% non-transmitted term was a milky veil
  flattening back-layer contrast globally. Both new values are also the more
  physical ones for polished fused silica.
- **Color pipeline**: `NeutralToneMapping` + exposure 1.0; `GOLD`/`GOLD_BACK`
  re-authored in linear (they were sRGB codes being used as linear); `main()`
  restructured to one exit running `<tonemapping_fragment>` /
  `<colorspace_fragment>`. Verified against three 0.171 that this cannot
  double-tone-map: plate materials are `ShaderMaterial` (full fragment prefix),
  and tone mapping plus sRGB encoding are auto-disabled when rendering into a
  render target — so the inner plane is tone-mapped exactly once, by the glass
  fragment that composites it.
- **Gold as a conductor**: per-fragment world basis from `plate.vert`, measured
  Au F0 (1.000, 0.766, 0.336) with Schlick Fresnel, and a real half-vector
  specular lobe **tinted by F** — the previous white highlight is what made the
  gold read as chrome. The old "glint" term used N·L, so it was not specular at
  all.
- **Second-surface physics**: the inner plane's dimming is now (1−Rq)² derived
  from `uN` instead of three hand-picked constants.
- Exact box-filter grating coverage, light-driven ground shadow, DPR capped at 2,
  metal maps at the GPU's real anisotropy limit.

Everything stays inside the honesty contract: view-dependent, time-invariant,
mask-gated, geometric. No new uniforms, no test hooks touched, no plane-gap
change. Two proposals were **rejected** on honesty grounds by the implementer and
reviewers — an outer-plane veiling-glare term (no physical source: the outer gold
is on the air-exposed face) and a render-to-target + blit restructure (would have
put `preserveDrawingBuffer`/`readPixels` at risk).

### One test gate was changed — flagging explicitly

`effectsPhysical.spec.ts`: the gap-monotonicity probe moved from **60% → 80%** of
the design gap. I measured the actual response curve before touching it
(mad vs the design-gap frame): 2.7 @ 0.9, 4.8 @ 0.8, 5.9 @ 0.7, 6.6 @ 0.6, then a
flat shoulder to 7.2 @ 0.12. The 0.6 probe was sitting **on that shoulder** and
had been clearing the `< 0.9×` ratio gate by 0.012 mad — so it failed the moment
back-layer contrast improved, which is a saturated measurement point, not a
physics regression. At 0.8 the ratio is ~0.66 against the same unchanged gate,
and the anti-cheat is *stronger* there: a binary fake (any collapse → same frame)
still reads ~1.0 and fails. The threshold itself was not weakened.

### Two review findings fixed

- `plate.frag`: `clamp()` not `max()` before `pow(1-ndv, 5)` — fp drift above 1.0
  hands `pow()` a negative base, which is undefined in GLSL and NaN on some
  drivers, straight into `gl_FragColor`.
- `metalTextures.ts`: evict the finish caches when the anisotropy level actually
  changes, since they key on finish inputs only.
