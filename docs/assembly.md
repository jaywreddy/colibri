# Assembling the box: foil and solder

The production box is six SINGLE plies of 2.25 mm fused quartz, each written on
one face, butt-jointed stained-glass style with copper foil and solder, with a
brass tube-and-rod hinge along the back top edge. There is no bonding and no
inner ply. Outer dimensions 32 × 32 × 35 mm; interior 27.5 × 27.5 × 30.5 mm.

Cut sizes of the six plies (as written on the 15 September plate):

| face | size | note |
|---|---|---|
| lid (top) | 32.0 × 32.0 | monogram; hinged |
| base (bottom) | 32.0 × 32.0 | solid gold |
| front, back | 32.0 × 30.5 | globe; Paris photo |
| left, right | 27.5 × 30.5 | beach; sunset |

Every die is written MIRRORED and is mounted **gold side in**: the pattern is
seen through its own quartz, and the gold sits on the inside surface of the
box where nothing touches it. The clear rim on each face is 3.64 mm from the
edge on both sides (the art keep-out), so foil and adhesive only ever meet bare
quartz.

## 0. Numbers

| | |
|---|---|
| ply | 2.25 mm fused quartz |
| foil | **1/4″ (6.35 mm)** copper tape; fold = (6.35 − 2.25) / 2 = **2.05 mm** onto each face |
| art keep-out | 3.64 mm from every edge, both faces: 1.6 mm of clear quartz between the foil edge and the pattern |
| hinge | 3/32″ brass tube (2.4 mm OD), 5 segments alternating body–lid–body–lid–body over 25.6 mm (80 % of the back edge), 1/16″ rod |
| solder | 60/40 or 63/37 (1 mm); lead-free 96/4 works but beads flatter and needs a hotter iron |
| iron | 80–100 W, temperature-controlled, 370–400 °C, ¼″ chisel tip |

Do not use 3/8″ tape on a single ply: its 3.64 mm fold lands exactly on the
art keep-out line, and any wander puts adhesive on the pattern.

## 1. How the faces meet

Plain butt joints, no rabbets:

- The four walls stand ON the base and the lid sits ON the wall tops
  (2.25 + 30.5 + 2.25 = 35 mm).
- Front and back run the full 32 mm width. The two sides (27.5 mm) stand
  between them, their ends butting the inside faces of the front and back.
- Foil on both pieces, then solder fills the V where two foiled edges meet.
  A stained-glass butt corner is not a glue joint: the solder bead IS the
  structure, and it is strong once it wraps the corner inside and out.

Because nothing self-registers, squareness comes from a jig and from tacking
before beading. That is the whole of section 3.

## 2. Foil

1. Clean each ply with IPA on a lint-free wipe; handle by the edges. The gold
   face is inside the keep-out rim, so a fingerprint on the rim is harmless and
   one on the pattern is a swab-and-IPA job, never a rub.
2. Lay 1/4″ tape along one edge on the OUTER (bare) face, its edge 2.05 mm in
   from the glass edge: the tape then folds over the 2.25 mm edge and 2.05 mm
   onto the gold-side face by itself. A paper card cut to 2.05 mm makes a good
   stop. ±0.5 mm is fine; the keep-out gives 1.6 mm of slack.
3. Fold: press the tape over the edge face, then down onto the inner face.
   Pinch the two folds tight at the corners of the ply so the tape does not
   pull off the edge when the next side is laid.
4. Burnish hard with a fid or bone folder on all three surfaces. Unburnished
   adhesive lifts under the iron and the seam pulls apart later.
5. Overlap tape ends by ~3 mm at the ply corners, never mid-edge. Trim the
   corner overlaps with a blade so the folds lie flat.
6. Flux lightly and **tin every foiled edge** now, with the ply flat on the
   bench: one pass of the iron with a little solder leaves a thin skin. Tinned
   foil cannot lift, and the seams later only need a bead.

Foil all six plies before any assembly. Check each one against a square: a ply
with a lifted corner of foil goes back to the fid, not into the box.

## 3. Jig, tack, square, bead

**The jig.** You need two true vertical faces at a right angle and a flat
floor. Any of these works:

- Two 1-2-3 blocks (or a machinist's angle plate) clamped to a flat plate at
  90°.
- A Lego frame: the base is exactly 4 studs (32 mm) square, so a pocket of
  bricks around a 4 × 4 stud area holds the base plate exactly, and a wall of
  bricks two or three high on two sides gives the right angle. Cheap, square
  to ~0.1 mm, and solder does not stick to it.
- A wooden corner jig: two strips of straight hardwood screwed to a board at
  90°, faced with masking tape so they do not scratch.

**Tacking.**

1. Base flat in the jig. Stand the front against one fence with its bottom
   edge on the base, gold side in. Stand a side against the other fence, its
   front end butting the inside of the front. A rubber band around the pair or
   a strip of masking tape across the outside corner holds them.
2. Check with a small machinist's square across the outside corner and that
   both walls sit flat on the base (no rock). Tack: one 2–3 mm dab of solder at
   the top and one at the bottom of the vertical corner, one at each end of
   each base seam. Tacks only; no beads yet. Let them cool to hand-warm.
3. Take the pair out of the jig, add the other side the same way (front against
   the fence, new side against the other fence), then the back. With four walls
   tacked, measure the two diagonals across the open top: equal within 0.3 mm
   is square. To correct, re-melt ONE tack and move that wall; never lever a
   cold joint.
4. Inside seams first, while the top is open: the four vertical inside corners
   and the four base seams, reaching in through the 27.5 mm opening with the
   chisel tip. A low fillet is enough; it is what carries the load.
5. Outside beads. Turn the box so the seam being run is horizontal and facing
   up (a vertical seam sags). Iron at about 45°, feed solder into the corner
   and pull steadily at about 1 cm/s; a good bead is a smooth half-round that
   spans both foils. Let a seam cool before turning to the next; keep the iron
   off the quartz faces (a scorch is permanent).

Quartz has near-zero thermal expansion, so the thermal-shock caution that
applies to soda-lime stained glass does not apply here; heat is the foil's
problem, not the glass's.

## 4. Lid and hinge

1. Foil and tin the lid like the others. Set it on the wall tops and check it
   sits flat and flush on all four sides before touching the hinge.
2. Cut 3/32″ tube into 5 segments of the length in `ASSEMBLY.md` (about 5 mm
   each, with the gap the sheet specifies so segments turn freely). Deburr, and
   thread them on the 1/16″ rod alternating body–lid–body–lid–body.
3. Lid closed, lay the tube string along the back top seam, centred. Flux and
   solder the three BODY segments (1st, 3rd, 5th) to the back wall's top bead,
   heating only those segments; the rod keeps everything in line.
4. Open the lid to 90° and solder the two LID segments to the lid's back edge
   bead. Work each one quickly so heat does not creep into the neighbour.
5. Cap the rod ends with a dab of solder on the outermost body segments. Test
   the swing; a sticky segment is heat that crept into a gap and is freed with
   the iron and a wipe.

## 5. Finish

1. Wash off all flux the same day: flux remover, or warm water with dish soap,
   then dry and wipe the outside faces with IPA. Flux left on copper corrodes
   green within weeks.
2. Optional patina (black or copper) rubbed on the beads and rinsed; it does
   not stain quartz but is a nuisance to wipe off it.
3. Polish the beads with a soft cloth; a trace of carnauba wax slows tarnish.
4. Ring liner: the interior is 27.5 × 27.5 × 30.5 mm. A 1 mm felt or suede
   liner over a 2 mm base pad, slotted for the band, leaves generous room; the
   liner also hides the base's solid gold if you would rather the ring sat on
   fabric.

## Tools

Fid or bone folder, hobby knife, 1/4″ copper foil, gel flux and brush, 60/40
solder (1 mm), temperature-controlled iron with ¼″ chisel tip, brass tip
cleaner, small machinist's square, jig (1-2-3 blocks, Lego, or a hardwood
corner), rubber bands, masking tape, IPA and lint-free wipes, tube cutter or
fine saw for 3/32″ brass, flux remover, optional patina and wax.
